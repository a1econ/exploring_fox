Sail the day.
Live the moment.

One- and two-day sailing trips to Aegina and Agistri, and tastings at a wine museum and a working brewery. Small groups, private or shared, planned around what you actually want from the day.

Plan your day See how a day runs

Sail. Taste. Celebrate.

Who takes you out

Maria plans and runs every trip herself, with Michalis at the helm. Both hold sailing licences and both are trained in first aid.

How many of you

Six to eight guests. Private, so it stays your group, or shared if you would rather meet people.

Where you go

Aegina and Agistri, an hour or so out of Mikrolimano. Longer routes are possible if you have the days.

One day, start to finish

How a day at sea runs

Nothing here is fixed. If you want to stay longer at one beach, or skip lunch for one more swim, the day changes — that is the point of a small boat.

09:30

Cast off from Mikrolimano

Coffee is on before the lines come off. Bags below, shoes off, and the harbour is behind you inside ten minutes.

11:00

Sails up, open water

The engine goes quiet and the Saronic opens out. Take the helm if you want to — most people do, once.

12:30

Anchor at Dragonera, Agistri

Pine down to the waterline and sand under four metres of water. The ladder goes down and stays down. There are sea toys aboard.

14:30

Lunch on deck

Cheese, cold cuts, something warm from the galley, and wine or a cold beer. Vegan menus and allergies are handled if you tell us when you book.

19:30

Back in, with the sun going down

The run home is the quiet part of the day. On a two-day trip this is where you stay out instead, and wake up on the water.

Three ways to spend a day

Inland

Wine tasting

The Costas Lazaridis Wine Museum: the ageing cellars, the hand-painted magnums, the cork tree, and one of the largest corkscrew collections in the world. It ends with a guided tasting paired with cheese, cold cuts and fresh bread.

In Athens

Beer tasting

Anastasiou Brewery, where Fotis has been brewing since 2010. Five beers — lager, pilsner, IPA, APA, saison, and a wild ale made with wild yeast and fruit — then a glass of the one you liked, with a board of Greek sausages. The pub shares the room with the brewery, so you drink while it is being made.
